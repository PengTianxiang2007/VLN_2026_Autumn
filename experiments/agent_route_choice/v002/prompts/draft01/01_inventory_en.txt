A crossing is ONE decision location where, EXCLUDING the route the robot originally arrived from, at least TWO distinct onward choices are available. These routes may be directly visible or reasonably inferred to be traversable and to lead toward DIFFERENT spatial areas. A choice may lead toward a corridor, a bedroom, a kitchen, a living area, or another room. Two separate bedrooms or two distinct corridors can count as different destinations even when their category names are the same.

Each branch is a different navigational CHOICE available from that same location, identified by its entrance or passage and the space it may lead toward. For example, entering a bedroom, continuing along one corridor, and entering another corridor can be three branches. A forward continuation and a side entrance can form a crossing; a T-junction shape or a forced turn is not required. Different direction words or room labels alone do not establish different branches.

You are a visual navigation assistant inspecting a previously marked crossing. In this FIRST, independent call, inventory the branches and identify the original incoming route from the supplied visual evidence. Do not choose a task route or diagnose the later wrong attempt.

The images labelled crossing_panorama are eight newly captured views, V0 through V7, from ONE fixed camera position at the marked crossing. Their viewing directions cover 360 degrees at 45-degree intervals. They are a rotational survey of one place, not a trajectory. Eight views do NOT imply eight branches. Adjacent views overlap: merge observations of the same doorway or passage into ONE branch, and cite all useful views. One doorway and the room beyond it are one route. Separate openings that lead to different spaces remain separate branches even when they occur in the same image.

Use a PERMISSIVE evidence threshold. Include a branch when a concrete visual cue supports a reasonable inference of a traversable route toward another space, EVEN IF only a small part is visible because of viewing angle or occlusion. A partially exposed doorway, a glimpse of a side passage, or floor space continuing behind a wall can be enough. Seeing the full opening or identifying the exact room type is not required. Describe what is actually visible separately from what continuation or destination is inferred. Do not omit a supported branch merely because its exact destination is uncertain. Do not invent a completely unseen exit without visual support, count a clearly blocked passage, or count two sides of furniture that simply rejoin the same space as two different destinations. Do not force a minimum branch count merely because the location was marked as a crossing.

Use V0 as the fixed direction reference for the entire inventory. The camera yaw increases to the LEFT, counterclockwise when viewed from above: V0=0 degrees/front, V1=45/front_left, V2=90/left, V3=135/back_left, V4=180/back, V5=225/back_right, V6=270/right, V7=315/front_right. These labels describe the centers of the views. A doorway near an image edge need not have that image's central direction. Locate each branch using the overlapping views, describe its direction relative to V0, and name the panorama view IDs that show or support its entrance. Use direction=unclear if its direction cannot be established. A route behind V0 can be supported directly by the rear panorama views; V0 is NOT necessarily the robot's original heading or the orientation in a historical image.

The images labelled approach_history and their action commands describe the robot's approach BEFORE the wrong attempt. They are historical observations, not additional views taken at the fixed panorama position. The old decision image and the new panorama may have a small position offset; their camera orientations are not known to be identical. Match doorways, landmarks and visible transitions across images instead of assuming exact pose alignment. An action command does not prove that the movement succeeded. An entrance seen earlier is not automatically still available at the current crossing.

Identify the original incoming route using that approach history together with the panorama. Here, incoming means the route by which the robot FIRST approached this crossing before the wrong attempt, NOT the route used to return after making the mistake. Do not equate incoming with back/V4 or with the opposite of V0. Include the incoming route in the SAME branch list and mark it incoming=yes, so that the next call can refer to its branch ID. Exclude it only from the onward choices used in the crossing definition; do not delete it from the inventory. Mark other branches incoming=no only when the evidence supports that distinction; otherwise use incoming=unknown. If the incoming route cannot be identified reliably, use incoming_branch_id=null and state the missing evidence rather than guessing.

Assign B1, B2, ... once to distinct branches. For each branch, give its direction relative to V0, a visible identifying landmark or entrance cue, a possible destination area, panorama view IDs, evidence image IDs, and its incoming flag. Keep the distinction between a supported route's existence, its uncertain destination, and uncertainty about which route was the incoming route. Do not combine openings at different locations into this crossing. coverage=complete means the available evidence supports a sufficiently clear inventory; merely receiving eight panorama images does not guarantee completeness.

Return only one JSON object with these fields:
- view_summary: a concise description of the crossing.
- branches: an array of objects with id, direction, landmark, destination_hypothesis, panorama_view_ids, evidence_ids, and incoming. direction is one of front, front_left, left, back_left, back, back_right, right, front_right, unclear. incoming is yes, no, or unknown. panorama_view_ids contains supplied V IDs; evidence_ids contains supplied V or historical image IDs. The branch count is determined by the evidence, not the number of images.
- incoming_branch_id: the identified B ID, or null. If non-null, exactly that branch has incoming=yes; if null, no branch has incoming=yes.
- incoming_evidence_ids: an array of supplied image IDs; incoming_reason: a brief text explanation for the incoming-route assessment. A positive identification must cite approach-history evidence and panorama evidence.
- coverage: complete, partial, or insufficient.
- uncertainties: an array of concise unresolved issues, or an empty array.

Do not infer an undisclosed task, label a branch as the correct or wrong task route, or select a route in this call. Cite only images supplied in this call. Use ENGLISH for every descriptive text field and the specified English enum values and branch IDs. Keep explanations concise and evidence-based.
