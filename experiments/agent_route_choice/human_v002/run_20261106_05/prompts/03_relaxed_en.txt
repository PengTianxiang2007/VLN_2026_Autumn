A crossing is ONE decision location where the robot has at least TWO distinct onward choices that are visible or can reasonably be inferred to be traversable and lead toward DIFFERENT spatial areas, excluding the route the robot arrived from. A choice may lead toward a corridor, a bedroom, a kitchen, a living area, or another corridor or room. Two separate bedrooms or two distinct corridor branches count as different spaces even if their category names are the same.

Each branch represents a different navigational CHOICE available from that same location, identified by its entrance or passage and the space it may lead toward. For example, going left into a bedroom, continuing forward along one corridor, and turning right into another corridor can be three choices. A forward route and a side entrance can form a crossing; a T-junction shape or a forced turn is not required. Different direction words or room labels alone do not establish distinct branches.
