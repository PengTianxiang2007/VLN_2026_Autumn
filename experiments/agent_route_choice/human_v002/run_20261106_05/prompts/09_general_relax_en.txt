A crossing is ONE decision location where the robot has at least TWO distinct onward choices that are visible or can reasonably be inferred to be traversable and lead toward DIFFERENT spatial areas, excluding the route the robot arrived from. A choice may lead toward a corridor, a bedroom, a kitchen, a living area, or another corridor or room. Two separate bedrooms or two distinct corridor branches count as different spaces even if their category names are the same.

Each branch represents a different navigational CHOICE available from that same location, identified by its entrance or passage and the space it may lead toward. For example, going left into a bedroom, continuing forward along one corridor, and turning right into another corridor can be three choices. A forward route and a side entrance can form a crossing; a T-junction shape or a forced turn is not required. For a visually suggested candidate, a possible continuation toward a different space is sufficient; confirming that the destination is distinct can be deferred to later review.

You are a visual navigation assistant at a previously identified crossing. The current camera view has been restored to the checkpoint view. Your task in this call is to inventory its available route choices using the definition above.

Use a very low evidence threshold: this call generates possible branch candidates, not confirmed routes. If there is any visible indication that movement could continue into another space, include that possibility in branches even when the indication is weak, ambiguous, or present in only one image. The destination and traversability may remain unconfirmed; state that uncertainty. A candidate need not be more likely than not: if a weak visible indication leaves a reasonable possibility of another route, list it as uncertain even when the non-route interpretation seems more likely. Prefer retaining a doubtful candidate over missing a possible route, but do not invent a candidate without any visible indication.

Use the current checkpoint image and the supplied earlier images and action commands. All directions must be relative to the camera orientation in current_view_id, even when evidence comes from an earlier view. Forward commands do not prove that movement succeeded. Historical images are not extra simultaneous camera views, and an entrance already passed is not automatically available at the current location.

Assign B1, B2, ... to distinct branches. For each, describe its relative direction, identifying landmark, possible destination area, and supporting image IDs. One doorway and the room beyond it form one route. Do not combine entrances at different locations into one crossing. A clearly blocked passage or a completely unseen exit without visual support is not an available choice. Merely going around opposite sides of furniture and returning to the same space does not establish two distinct destination choices.

You may include the identifiable incoming route in the inventory with incoming=yes to support later error analysis, but do not count it toward the crossing's two onward choices. Otherwise use incoming=no or unknown as supported. A route behind the camera needs support from the supplied history. Keep uncertainty about exact destinations separate from whether a supported branch exists. Do not force a minimum branch count when there is no supporting evidence. Coverage=complete means the supplied views support a sufficiently clear inventory, not that a panoramic survey was performed.

Do not choose a route, diagnose the previous mistake, or infer a hidden task in this call. Give concise visual descriptions and cite only supplied image IDs. Return only the requested JSON object. Use ENGLISH for all descriptive text, together with the specified English enum values and branch IDs.
